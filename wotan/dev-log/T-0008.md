# T-0008: Phase 2: Full turbulent curved-pipe solver + Ito friction factor validation

**Status**: BLOCKED | **Size**: L
**Phase**: —

## Context

With the Van Driest mixing-length model validated for straight-pipe flow (T-0007),
this phase re-enables curvature and runs the full turbulent Dean flow solver.

The key physics change: at Dean numbers above the laminar-turbulent transition
(De_c ~ 1100 for Collins & Dennis curvature δ ≈ 0.05), the turbulent model should
yield substantially different (and more physically realistic) W and secondary flow
patterns compared to the laminar solver.

Primary validation target: Ito (1959) friction factor correlation for turbulent
flow in curved pipes.

## Ito (1959) Friction Factor

The turbulent friction factor for curved pipes (Ito 1959):

```
f_c = 0.304 × Re^{−0.25} × (R/a)^{−0.05}   [smooth wall, fully turbulent]
```

Straight-pipe Blasius: `f_0 = 0.316 × Re^{−0.25}`

Enhancement ratio: `f_c / f_0 = 0.304/0.316 × (R/a)^{-0.05} ≈ 0.96 × (R/a)^{-0.05}`

For R/a = 20: `f_c / f_0 ≈ 0.96 × 20^{-0.05} ≈ 0.96 × 0.857 ≈ 0.82`

Note: Ito finds curved pipes have *lower* friction than straight at the same Re in
the turbulent regime (centrifugal force stabilises the boundary layer), in contrast
to the laminar regime where Dean number increases resistance.
